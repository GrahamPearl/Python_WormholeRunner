6. Physics

Movement, gravity, wrap-around and collisions, in real code.

Learning intentions

  • use speed, time and direction to move things
  • understand how gravity is calculated from mass and distance
  • understand wrap-around using the modulo operator
  • check for collisions using distance

Success criteria: I can…

  • explain x = x + v * dt
  • say what happens to gravity when the distance doubles
  • explain why the game uses % for wrap-around
  • describe how two circles are tested for a collision

1. Movement: position, speed and time

Speed tells us how far something moves in one second. In one frame it moves speed × dt. The new position is the old position plus that move. This is the same idea as distance = speed × time from science class.

3. Gravity

Isaac Newton found that the pull between two objects gets stronger with mass and weaker with distance. It gets weaker very quickly, with the square of the distance:

pull = G × mass ÷ distance²

Double the distance and the pull is four times weaker. In the game, G is the setting gravity. Two safety tricks stop the pull from becoming silly: a softening number added to the distance (so a ship right on top of a rock is not pulled with infinite force) and a cap (a maximum).

4. Wrap-around: the modulo operator

The map has no walls. Fly off the right side and you appear on the left. The trick is the modulo operator %, which gives the remainder after division. If the world is 1280 wide, then 1290 % 1280 is 10. The position wraps back to the start.

Wrapping creates a new problem. A ship at the far right and a rock at the far left are really close together, even though their numbers are far apart. The delta method finds the shortest way, even if it goes across the edge:

If the gap is more than half the world, going the other way is shorter, so it takes away one world width. Every collision, every pull and every alien chase uses delta.

5. Collisions: circles and Pythagoras

The game treats most things as circles. Two circles touch when the distance between their centres is less than the two radii added together. The distance comes from Pythagoras' theorem (math.hypot does it in one go):

When two rocks collide and "split when rocks collide" is off, they bounce. The bounce uses mass: a heavy rock changes a light rock's direction more than the other way round.

6. Steering a missile

The homing missile turns toward its target, but only a little each frame, so it curves. The clever line is the one that finds the shortest turn (left or right) between two angles:

(wanted - heading + π) % (2π) - π always gives an answer between −180° and +180°. Positive means turn one way, negative the other.

Try it: test the physics

  1. In the Game Editor, set Gravity strength to 0. Fly close to a rock. What changes?
  2. Set Drift damping to 0. What happens after you are pulled by a rock? Why?
  3. Fly off the right edge. Where do you come out? Which function made that happen?

Check your understanding

1. A ship moves at 300 pixels per second. How far does it move in one frame at 60 frames per second?
300 ÷ 60 = 5 pixels.
2. A rock pulls with strength 80 at 100 pixels. How strong is the pull at 200 pixels?
Double the distance gives one quarter of the pull: 20.
3. What is 1290 % 1280?
10.
4. Why is World.delta needed as well as World.wrap?
Wrap moves things back onto the map. Delta works out the true shortest distance between two things, even across the edge.
5. Two circles have radii 20 and 30. The centres are 45 apart. Do they touch?
Yes: 20 + 30 = 50, and 45 is less than 50.
