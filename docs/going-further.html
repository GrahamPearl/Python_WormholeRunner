11. Going further

Multiplayer, safety and ideas to take the game further.

Learning intentions

  • get a first idea of how two computers can play one game
  • learn the words: network, client, server, protocol
  • think about staying safe online
  • find ideas to extend the game

Success criteria: I can…

  • explain why one computer is the 'host'
  • explain what a snapshot is
  • list two rules for staying safe when playing online

Two computers, one game

When two people play together, both screens must show the same rocks and aliens. If each computer ran its own game, they would soon disagree. So one computer, the host, runs the real game. The other computer, the joiner, sends what keys it is pressing and draws pictures of the world the host sends back.

Finding a game

A host shouts "I'm here!" to everyone on the local network when asked. This is called a broadcast. The joiner's search sends a short question and listens for answers:

Snapshots

About 30 times a second the host builds a snapshot: a list of where everything is. It is a dictionary turned into text (JSON) and sent over the network:

Smooth movement

Snapshots arrive in jumps, but we want smooth movement. The joiner interpolates: it slides things from their old position to their new one. For its own ship it predicts: it moves the ship straight away using the same rules as the host, then gently corrects if the host disagrees.

Why TCP?

Computers send data in different ways. TCP guarantees everything arrives, in order. UDP is faster but may lose messages. The game uses TCP for play and UDP only for the 'who is hosting?' question.

Staying safe

  • Only play with people you know, on a trusted network such as your school or home.
  • Never share your address, school or full name in a game.
  • Your teacher or caregiver should know who you are playing with.
  • If someone makes you uncomfortable, stop playing and tell a trusted adult.

Programmers have a responsibility too: this game never asks for personal information, and your credits are saved only on your own computer.

Ideas to extend the game

  • A new alien with its own think method (a Shielder that protects other aliens?).
  • A new ship with an ability that heals nearby ships or slows time.
  • A high-score table saved in a file.
  • Your own art and sound: replace the generated pictures in assets/images.
  • A level editor that places rocks by hand.
  • Show your credits and scores on a web page (see the next page).

Using an AI helper well

An AI assistant can write code, but it is only a helper. You must read what it writes, test it, and be able to explain it. Good prompts are small, clear and ask for one thing at a time. The tutorial page shows examples.

Try it: explain it

Pick any code box on this site. Write three sentences explaining it to a friend who has never programmed. Then ask a classmate to find anything you got wrong.

Check your understanding

1. Why does one computer act as the host?
So there is one true copy of the game, and both players see the same thing.
2. What is a snapshot?
A list of where everything is at one moment, sent from host to joiner.
3. What does interpolation do?
It slides things smoothly between the positions from two snapshots.
4. Give two safety rules for online games.
For example: only play with people you know; do not share personal information.
5. Why must you test code an AI helper gives you?
It can contain mistakes. You are responsible for checking it works and understanding it.
